# AWM Standard PPO Baseline v1

The machine-readable source of truth is `configs/ppo_baseline_v1.json`.

## Scope

PPO v1 is a **single-policy, strictly on-policy** learned comparator for the AWM irrigation problem. It is intentionally simpler than RCWA-RL: PPO optimizes a fixed scalar expected-return surrogate and is evaluated afterward under the same Risk Contract v1. It does not contain a distributional yield critic, a CVaR optimizer, a dual variable, budget-monotonic value regularization, population transfer, CPO coupling, or LRMB mechanisms.

The implementation was informed by the read-only LRMB `exp/ppo` branch only for two engineering principles: strict single-policy/on-policy PPO and correct hierarchical gate-plus-active-amount likelihood accounting. AWM has no runtime dependency on LRMB and does not copy LRMB's nitrogen action or study-specific reward.

## Environment and conditioning

- actor observation: the frozen 79D AWM state;
- nitrogen: fixed, never an action;
- postplant water upper bound: 495 mm (W100 policy quota);
- fixed preplant water: 45 mm, included in reported seasonal irrigation but not in the controllable water cost;
- requested protection target: `eta in {0.90, 0.95, 0.98}`, already present as `yield_target_fraction` in the observation;
- training weather: ERA5 2000-2017 only;
- validation weather: ERA5 2018-2022 only;
- station 2023-2025: locked final test and inaccessible to PPO training/selection.

PPO is not trained under separate W60/W80/W100 quotas. It receives the common W100 **upper bound** and learns how much of that controllable water to use. W60/W80/W100 remain fixed agricultural comparators.

## Hierarchical irrigation policy

The actor represents

\[
\pi(g,z\mid s)=\mathrm{Bernoulli}(g\mid s)\,\mathrm{Normal}(z\mid s)^g.
\]

`g=0` is exact no irrigation. Only when `g=1` is the Gaussian latent active, with

\[
u=\frac{\tanh z+1}{2}\in(0,1),
\]

and the existing WaterBudgetController maps `u` to the currently feasible physical irrigation interval.

The behavior record stored by PPO is the sampled gate and **pre-tanh latent**. PPO likelihood ratios are computed from those sampled variables. Executed DSSAT irrigation, remaining-budget projection, and 0.1-mm execution canonicalization remain separate audit quantities and are never substituted into the behavior likelihood.

Actor architecture:

```text
79 -> Linear 256 -> LayerNorm -> Tanh
   -> Linear 128 -> LayerNorm -> Tanh
   -> irrigation Bernoulli gate
   -> state-dependent amount Normal mean/log_std
```

The critic is an independent `79 -> 256 -> 128 -> 1` network with the same activation/normalization pattern. Actor and critic parameters are not shared.

## PPO scalar surrogate

The DSSAT environment remains reward-free. PPO owns a benchmark-specific scalar reward.

At each step:

\[
r_t^{water}=-\frac{I_t}{495}.
\]

At terminal time:

\[
R=\frac{Y}{Y_{ref}(w)}.
\]

The terminal violation term is

\[
r_T^{viol}=\begin{cases}
0,&R\ge\eta,\\
-(1+\eta-R),&R<\eta.
\end{cases}
\]

With `gamma=1`,

\[
G=-\frac{I_{policy}}{495}-\mathbf 1[R<\eta](1+\eta-R).
\]

For a **single trajectory**, every target-feasible episode has return in `[-1,0]`, while every target-infeasible episode has return below `-1`. This is a trajectory-ranking property only. PPO maximizes expected scalar return, so outcomes across weather years can compensate in expectation. The surrogate therefore does **not** impose a hard distribution-level constraint and does **not** guarantee

\[
LCVaR_{0.20}(R\mid\eta)\ge\eta.
\]

That gap is intentional: PPO is the scalar expected-return learned baseline, while formal risk success is evaluated separately under Risk Contract v1.

## Balanced on-policy rollout

One PPO update collects exactly one full Cartesian cycle:

\[
18\text{ train years}\times3\eta=54\text{ complete episodes}.
\]

Each episode has 125 decisions, so one rollout contains exactly

\[
54\times125=6750
\]

transitions. Every weather-target cell appears once per update; order is deterministically shuffled from `(training seed, update index)`.

A rollout carries one `policy_version`. After one PPO update the version increments. A rollout generated by an older version is rejected rather than reused.

## Optimizer contract

- optimizer: Adam;
- learning rate: `1e-4`;
- Adam betas: `(0.9, 0.999)`;
- Adam epsilon: `1e-8`;
- gamma: `1.0`;
- GAE lambda: `1.0`;
- PPO clip: `0.2`;
- epochs per rollout: `10`;
- minibatch: `450` (15 minibatches per epoch);
- value coefficient: `0.5`;
- entropy coefficient: `0.0`;
- global gradient norm cap: `0.5`;
- full-rollout advantage standardization;
- unclipped critic MSE;
- no reward scaling.

`gamma=1` makes early and late irrigation costs comparable and preserves the exact undiscounted episode-return interpretation. `GAE lambda=1` is frozen because the main crop-yield protection signal occurs at the end of the 125-day episode; using `lambda<1` would exponentially attenuate the direct terminal consequence for early-season actions.

## State normalization

The 79D observation is normalized with running mean/variance estimated from **training observations only**. The normalizer is frozen within an entire 54-episode rollout, then updated from the collected training observations before the PPO update. It is frozen for validation and final evaluation. Reward scaling is disabled.

## Fixed learned-method interaction budget

Per seed:

\[
200\text{ updates}\times54\text{ episodes}=10{,}800\text{ training episodes},
\]

and

\[
10{,}800\times125=1{,}350{,}000\text{ environment decisions}.
\]

Across five seeds this is 54,000 training episodes and 6.75 million environment decisions.

This is the common **maximum learned-method training interaction budget**. RCWA-RL and any other learned comparator must receive the same maximum environment-training budget per seed. Any additional DSSAT trajectory used to update learned parameters, risk estimators, dual variables, or auxiliary trainable modules counts toward the budget. Validation trajectories do not count as training data, but every learned method must use the same candidate-checkpoint cadence and validation budget.

## Seeds and checkpoint semantics

Formal PPO seeds are:

```text
11, 21, 31, 41, 51
```

There is no seed selection.

Two checkpoint classes are deliberately separated:

- **recovery checkpoint**: saved every update for exact restart only;
- **scientific candidate checkpoint**: saved only at updates `10,20,...,200` and eligible for validation/model selection.

Recovery checkpoints at updates outside the candidate cadence can never become selector candidates. This prevents crash-recovery frequency from increasing checkpoint-selection multiplicity.

Each seed therefore exposes exactly 20 scientific candidates. Every candidate is evaluated on exactly 15 cells (5 validation years × 3 eta), giving 300 validation episodes per seed.

## Validation and final evaluation

Training remains stochastic. Validation and final evaluation are **deterministic**.

For the Bernoulli gate, deterministic action uses the threshold `p(irrigate)>=0.5`. For active irrigation amount, the pre-tanh Normal mean is transformed to `amount_fraction`.

This means the formal empirical risk variable remains weather-year outcome under one deterministic policy. Policy-action Monte Carlo noise is not mixed into weather risk. Validation and final evaluation do not consume the PPO training RNG state.

Checkpoint selection follows `awm-learned-method-evaluation-v1`: one candidate checkpoint per training seed must jointly serve all three eta levels. All five seeds remain independent replicates.

The station years 2023-2025 remain locked until the algorithm, hyperparameters, five seeds, candidate schedule, deterministic evaluation mode, and selected checkpoint for every seed are frozen.

- Git commit;

- training seed;
- Python version;
- PyTorch version;
- CUDA runtime version;
- device name;
- DSSAT executable SHA256.

## Fair-comparison boundary for RCWA-RL

The PPO actor architecture, hierarchical action parameterization, observation normalization, agricultural protocol, weather/eta schedule, candidate cadence, deterministic evaluation mode, and environment-training interaction budget should remain matched in RCWA-RL unless a difference is explicitly part of the proposed algorithm. RCWA-RL may add risk-specific critics, estimators, constraints, or dual variables, but it should not receive a larger actor or more environment data by default.
